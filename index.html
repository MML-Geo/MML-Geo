About MML-GEO

Geomaterials such as granular materials, rocks, and concrete derive their macroscopic response from evolving microstructures. This makes their behavior difficult to predict with classical continuum models, which are computationally efficient but often rely on phenomenological parameters only inspired by the microstructure. Multiscale modelling and computational homogenization provide a principled alternative by coupling macroscale simulations with representative elementary volumes, as in FE² or FEM×DEM approaches. Their broader use, however, remains limited by the repeated solution of computationnaly expensive auxiliary problems and by the limited interpretability of the macro–micro relationship, which is typically reconstructed numerically at each time step. MML-Geo addresses this bottleneck by combining constitutive laws formulation through stochastic homogenization with physics-informed machine learning. Its goal is to identify a compact and physically meaningful set of microstructural state variables governing the macroscopic behavior, and to build constitutive surrogates that are thermodynamically consistent, uncertainty-aware, and readily integrable into finite-element simulations for geotechnical analysis. The project aims to (1) identify compact internal variable representations of evolving geomaterial microstructures and determine when they are sufficient to predict constitutive behavior; (2) develop thermodynamically consistent, uncertainty aware, and computationally efficient data driven constitutive models; and (3) transfer these surrogate models to large scale structural simulations through stochastic homogenization and evaluate their performance on representative engineering demonstrators. By combining data science with multiscale modeling, MML Geo enables faster micro to macro simulations, robust uncertainty assessment, and practical validation on earthen dams and tunnels.
